Real yields and policy

A nominal yield splits into a real yield (TIPS) and breakeven inflation. Beside it: the effective fed funds rate, and how far the 2-year sits from it — a quick read on where the market thinks policy is heading.

Credit spreads

Option-adjusted spreads over Treasuries for US investment-grade and high-yield corporate bonds — what lenders charge for default risk, and the quickest gauge of risk appetite in the credit market.

Method and sources

Tenor is a static page. A scheduled GitHub Actions job pulls public data once each weekday evening, writes one JSON file, and redeploys the site. Nothing runs on a server and no API key is involved.

Sources

  • Yield curve — U.S. Department of the Treasury, Daily Treasury Par Yield Curve Rates (1M to 30Y), one CSV per year since 2000.
  • Real yield and breakeven — FRED series DFII10 (10Y TIPS) and T10YIE (10Y breakeven inflation).
  • Policy rate — FRED series DFF, the effective federal funds rate.
  • Credit spreads — ICE BofA US High Yield (BAMLH0A0HYM2) and US Corporate (BAMLC0A0CM) option-adjusted spreads, via FRED.

Definitions

  • Spreads are differences of yields, in basis points (1 bp = 0.01%).
  • Curve shape — inverted when 10Y − 3M is below −10 bp, flat within ±10 bp around zero on 10Y − 2Y, steep above +100 bp, otherwise normal.
  • Inversion episode — a run of at least ten consecutive sessions with 10Y − 3M below zero; gaps of up to five positive sessions are bridged.
  • Forwards — f(t₁,t₂) = [(1+y₂)^t₂ / (1+y₁)^t₁]^(1/(t₂−t₁)) − 1, with par yields standing in for zero rates. A true zero curve would need bootstrapping; on a normal curve the difference is a few basis points.
  • Percentile — share of observed days in the available history with a lower value than today.

Limits

  • Treasury publishes after the close, so the latest curve is the previous business day's during US hours.
  • FRED values are carried forward at most five sessions to line up with Treasury days; older gaps stay blank.
  • Nothing here is a forecast. An inverted curve is a historical regularity, not a timing signal.
